About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Industrial and Manufacturing Engineering (Operations Research dual title) at The Pennsylvania State University, co-advised by Dr. Paul Griffin (Lucas Professor; Director, Penn State Consortium on Substance Use & Addiction) and Dr. Soundar Kumara. My comprehensive examination is complete and I plan to defend in late Summer / early Fall 2026.

My research sits at the intersection of healthcare data analytics, statistical modeling, and decision-making about health. The recurring question I encounter is how to translate observational health data into intervention decisions that hold up when we look at who is being missed and what those decisions cost.

Research

My dissertation, Promoting Equity in Mental Health and Addressing Diseases of Despair, is built as three connected papers on a TriNetX Diamond Network electronic health-records cohort of 513,070 adults across 876 ZIP-3 areas linked to community-level social determinants of health.

Paper 1

Multilevel SDoH analysis · BMJ Open (under review)

Multilevel logistic regression (R / glmmTMB). Null ICC 4.0%; individual demographics and insurance explained 47.1% of between-region variance, with community-level SDoH adding 15.0%. Medicaid enrollment was the strongest individual risk factor; female sex was protective; area-level economic distress raised odds after adjustment.

Paper 2

Interpretable ML + fairness audit · in progress

Logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost with SHAP explanation, calibration, decision-curve analysis, and a stratified fairness audit by sex, race, ethnicity, age, and insurance. A model clinicians can read and that gets validated at the subgroup level before it leaves my hands.

Paper 3

Cost-effectiveness microsimulation · CHERISH Pilot Grant (PI, under review)

Individual-level microsimulation comparing Medicaid primary-care screening plus targeted telehealth expansion against usual care, fed by the Paper 2 classifier. Medicaid-payer perspective with probabilistic sensitivity analysis and cost-effectiveness acceptability curves. Mentor: Bruce Schackman (Weill Cornell).
